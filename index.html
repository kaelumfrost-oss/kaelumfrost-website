You are not alone in this

You have always been too many things at once.

You were told that was a problem to solve.

Pick one. Stay in your lane. Be consistent. Build a personal brand around the one thing the market will pay for. So you tried. You shrank yourself into a category. And it worked just enough to keep you trapped, and never enough to feel like you.

Here is what no one told you: the fragmentation was never the flaw. The instruction to fragment was the flaw. You are not a collection of unrelated interests competing for a niche. You are one person, and every interest is a facet of the same reality — yours.

The musicians who became movements were never the most marketable. They were the most themselves.

You are not behind.
You are undecided.

§05 / WHO
About

I spent years trying to become someone the market would approve of.

It half-worked. Which is the worst outcome — successful enough to keep doing it, empty enough to know it wasn't mine.

The day it changed wasn't dramatic. I just noticed that everyone I actually admired had done the opposite. They didn't find a gap in the market. They became so completely themselves that a market formed around them.

That's when the belief I build everything on stopped being abstract: reality reflects identity. You don't get what you chase. You get what you are. So the entire game is who you decide to be — and then refusing to negotiate it down.

I'm not a guru. I don't have it figured out. I'm a person building a reality out loud — through music, through strategy, through a room full of people who were also told they were too much.

If that's you, you already belong here. The only thing left is to come inside.
